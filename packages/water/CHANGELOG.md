# @earthkit/water

## 0.1.0

### Minor Changes

- 22e6e50: Water: a `'minimal'` quality level for devices without a GPU (64 texels simulated every third frame, no wave mesh), picked by `quality: 'auto'` when the first frame shows a software renderer (`isSoftwareRenderer`: SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft's Basic Render Driver, a fallback adapter). New `adaptiveQuality` option (default true): while frames average slower than 30 a second for 2 s, the quality steps down a level (high, low, minimal) and the console says so. `water.quality` now reports the level drawn now, `water.setQuality(level)` changes it at run time, and `WaveSurface.setLevel` changes the simulation's size, pace and detail.
- 4dc729f: Water: four-point buoyancy. `water.createBuoyancy(hull)` (React: `useWaveBuoyancy`) floats a hull of `lengthMeters` by `beamMeters` at a heading on four probes at its corners and gives its `heightMeters`, `pitchRadians` and `rollRadians`, followed with a lag of `responseSeconds` (`WAVE_BUOYANCY_RESPONSE_SECONDS`, 0.35 s) so it rides the swell rather than every ripple; `draftMeters` sinks it to its waterline. `hullCorners` gives the corners.
- b163e90: Water: close-up detail and sun glitter. The ripples finer than the smallest wave tile, the rest of Cox & Munk's slope variance from the wind, are drawn as normal detail near the camera (`WAVE_DETAIL_SCALES`; `WaveSurface.setSurfaceMeanSquareSlope`), and the roughness gives up what they draw. On the wave mesh, the sun's glint off the ripples too fine to draw glitters: cells about a pixel wide flash in turn along the glint, instead of one smooth, milky highlight.
- b4ebf17: Water: the wave mesh reflects with water's index of refraction (`WATER_INDEX_OF_REFRACTION`, 1.34) and glows where the atmosphere's sun shines through the crests towards the camera, turquoise from the water's optics: `computeWaterOptics` and `water.state` give the new `crestTransmittance` per band (sunlight scattered once through `WATER_CREST_THICKNESS_METERS` of water). The mesh's shading callback receives the view at each fragment (`WaveMeshView`: normal, direction to the camera, wave height) and may return an `emissive` colour.
- 52c976d: Water: the sea mesh reads the ground under it from a map of the terrain drawn from above (a depth cache), so it draws only over water and at the water's own level, the height of its nearest shore (found by a jump flood on the GPU): lakes below sea level, a terrain whose sea sits on the geoid or over a drawn seafloor, polders and low coasts come out right. Its waves flatten at the shore and, with `depthFromElevation`, calm where they would break in the shallows, with the seafloor showing through. Probes report the water's level and whether the point is on land (`onWater`).

  Globe: `terrain.renderTiles(renderer, camera, target, material, clearColor)` draws the terrain's tiles alone with one node material, for a plugin's own view of the ground; tile meshes sit on `TERRAIN_LAYER`.

- ce8ef3d: Water: under WebGPU the waves are simulated in compute shaders (`WaveComputeField`), at no CPU cost, and draw foam on the crests that break: the Jacobian of the choppy displacement, thresholded so the foam covers the whitecap share (Monahan) and giving way to an even pallor where the waves fade out. WebGL2 keeps the CPU simulation. `WaveField` now writes every cascade into one array texture (`field.texture`, `cascade.slopes`). Globe: `PluginFrame.renderer`, the renderer drawing the frame, for a plugin's own GPU work.
- a564b48: Water: foam lasts. On the GPU each wave tile keeps the foam its breaking crests leave, fading over `WAVE_FOAM_LIFETIME_SECONDS` (4 s), so foam trails behind the waves that broke; crests breaking at once are `WAVE_BREAKING_SHARE` of the whitecap coverage, the rest is the foam they left. Near the camera the foam breaks into bubbly cells that open into a web as it thins.
- 532bcbb: Water: the sea mesh puts its detail where the camera looks and goes finer at the water. Its rings centre ahead of the camera along the view (`lookAhead`: half the way to where the view meets the sea, at most four camera heights), so the finest lie in view rather than under and behind it, still snapping to their cells. Two more rings (`WAVE_MESH_LEVELS` 10) take the innermost cell down to 1.6 cm right at the water (`WAVE_MESH_MINIMUM_CELL_METERS`), where the short chop becomes geometry, with the same reach; about 146 000 triangles instead of 118 000.
- c406142: New package `@earthkit/water`: a physically based sea as a plugin (`new Water()`, `<Water>` from `@earthkit/water/react`). Wherever the terrain is water, it shades over the imagery and terrain styles: the colour from the water's optics (seawater, phytoplankton and dissolved matter absorption and scattering, from `chlorophyllMilligramsPerCubicMeter` and `salinityPartsPerThousand`), the gloss from the wind after Cox & Munk (so under `<Atmosphere>` the sea mirrors the sky and the glint widens with the wind), whitecaps after Monahan, the seafloor through shallow water (`depthFromElevation`), and a map colour on the flat map. Presets: `map`, `calm`, `breeze`, `open-ocean`, `storm`, `coastal`. Settings update uniforms only.

  `@earthkit/globe` exports `terrainFlatMorph`, 0 on the globe and 1 on the flat map, for terrain shadings that fade to a map colour.

- ef0648c: Water: a `quality` option for phones, read once. `'auto'` (the default) picks `'low'` on a touch screen and `'high'` otherwise (`detectWaterQuality`). Low simulates the waves at 128 texels a side instead of 256 and steps them every other frame, lays the mesh with 32 cells a ring instead of 48 (about 66 000 triangles instead of 146 000, same reach), and leaves out the close-up ripples, the sun's glitter and the foam's cells (`WATER_QUALITY_LEVELS`). `WaveSurface` takes `computeGridSize`, `detail` and `framesPerSimulationStep`; the mesh's innermost cell now follows its resolution.
- f0208e1: Water: under WebGPU, a displaced sea mesh near the camera (geometry clipmaps, finest at the camera's feet, morphing between rings, flattening into the terrain's sea at its edge), moved by the waves' height and choppy displacement computed on the GPU (`waveMesh`, default true). Wave probes sample the sea as the mesh draws it, on the GPU with an asynchronous readback, to float a boat (`water.createProbe()`, `useWaveProbe` in React). The waves are seeded (`waveSeed`), and `waveSurface.timeSeconds` can be set to show the same sea on several WebGPU clients.

  Globe: terrain shadings get a `mask` slot (`NodeMaterial.maskNode`), to discard the ground where a plugin draws geometry of its own.

- 606141e: Water: a storm that looks like one. From 8 m/s of wind the waves steepen (choppiness 1 to 1.3 by 20 m/s, `choppinessForWind`), the foam left behind lies in streaks along the wind, longer as it rises (`WAVE_FOAM_STREAK_STRETCH_PER_METER_PER_SECOND`), and from 15 m/s spume whitens the faces around the breaking crests. A new `cloudCover` setting (0 to 1; 0.9 in the `storm` preset, 0 in the others) dims the sun's glow through the crests, its glitter and the light under the waves. Presets gain a `cloudCover` value, and `WaterState` reports it.
- 5d7dfe4: The surf zone: where the surf breaks, whitewater trails each breaking crest and thins until the next, over a lasting
  sheen of foam, all broken into the whitecaps' patches, and the water is clouded with stirred-up sand (the seafloor's
  colour). `WaveMeshGround` gains `surfZone` for custom mesh shadings, and `WaveSurface.patternFoam` breaks any foam
  density into patches.
- 6e57ab7: Surf near the shore: on the wave mesh, a train of waves runs straight at the nearest shore, shortens and steepens over
  the shallows and breaks with foam at 0.78 of the depth. New settings `surfHeightMeters` (default 0.6 of the wind sea's
  significant wave height, 0 for none) and `surfPeriodSeconds` (default the wind sea's peak period). Without depth data
  the wind's waves now also calm towards the shore, over a gently sloping beach. The depth cache keeps the distance and
  direction to the nearest shore (`WaveDepthCache.sampleShore`). New exports: `peakPeriodSeconds`, `deepWavenumber`,
  `shallowWavenumber`, `shoalingCoefficient`, `surfPhase` and the `SURF_*` constants.
- d51969a: Waves: the physical presets draw an FFT ocean near the camera. A JONSWAP spectrum from the wind (`windSpeedMetersPerSecond`, new `windDirectionDegrees` and `fetchKilometers`) with gravity-capillary dispersion from the water (new `temperatureCelsius`, and salinity, through density and surface tension) shapes four tiles (800 m, 110 m, 15 m, 2.1 m) simulated on the CPU every frame while the camera is below 30 km and off the flat map and laid on a plane under the camera with 64-bit offsets. They tilt the sea's normal and fade out where too small to draw, the roughness taking their slopes back, so the glint stays right at every distance. `waves: false` turns them off; `state.significantWaveHeightMeters` reports the sea state. New exports: `WaveField`, `WaveSurface`, the spectrum functions.
- 6951885: WebGL2 follows the quality level: the CPU's wave simulation is a quarter of the level's grid (64 texels a side at high, 32 at low and minimal, `cpuGridSizeFor`) and steps every `framesPerSimulationStep` frames, and `adaptiveQuality` judges the frame time there too. Each wave's random phase is now hashed from the seed and its wavenumber, so every quality level and device draws the same long waves for the same seed (the sea for a given seed changes once with this release).

### Patch Changes

- 01d77b0: Water: `adaptiveQuality` is gentler. It only judges frames while the wave mesh is drawn, waits 10 s after a start or a change (shaders compile, tiles stream) and 5 s of slow frames before acting, and lowers high to low only, never to `'minimal'`: a busy GPU kept dropping to the level without a wave mesh, so the waves seemed gone. `'minimal'` is still picked for a software renderer under `quality: 'auto'`, or by choice (`water.setQuality('minimal')`).
- b88ad52: Less CPU per frame: the depth cache redraws for changed tiles at most once a second (`WAVE_DEPTH_CACHE_REFRESH_SECONDS`,
  was four times a second). Each redraw draws every tile again, a few milliseconds of the frame, and tiles change nearly
  every frame while the camera moves. A move of the mesh still redraws it at once.
- be1d4d9: The sea mesh no longer floats in a plateau metres above the sea near coasts, with stretched walls where two shores met.
  Its level now comes from the terrain's own water level (`terrainWaterLevelMeters`: sea level on a raster DEM, the drawn
  height under a quantized mesh's mask), taken only from flat water and spread from the nearest flat water to the coast.
  On a flattened Terrarium sea the old rule kept only the coast's climbing triangles, 4 to 11 m up at Étretat. The level is
  stored over sea level rather than over the wave plane, so its half floats keep millimetres instead of up to 12 cm. Land
  within half a cache texel of the coast is no longer cut away under the mesh.
- 51d6e93: Water: the foam's bubbly cells and wind streaks come from a small tiling texture built once (`createFoamTexture`, 256² texels, shared), read in one lookup, instead of cellular and fractal noise worked out for every pixel. That noise cost about 70 % of the sea's frame: on a CPU renderer a frame of the storm drops from about 3 s to 0.9 s, and GPUs gain in proportion. The look is the same.
- be1177b: No more plateaus and lines in the sea near coasts: the water's level near a shore is now the lowest water drawn at or
  above sea level within about a kilometre (it was 64 m), so a coarse terrain tile whose coastal triangles climb under
  its water mask no longer lifts the sea into a step with a stretched wall; and the one-texel cracks between terrain
  tiles of different detail are filled before the shore pass, so they no longer cut green lines out of the sea.
- f3a705d: `water.setQuality` now changes everything at run time: the wave mesh's resolution (its rings are laid again) and the
  close-up detail (ripples, foam cells, glitter), which the shaders now branch on instead of leaving out when built. A
  level picked with `setQuality` stays: it turns `adaptiveQuality` off, which could otherwise take it back down.
- 4964cce: Fix the sea mesh on real coastlines: each shore takes its level from the lowest water drawn at or above sea level
  within four texels, so water no longer floats in patches over beaches whose triangles climb under the water mask; the
  surf's dispersion no longer overflows to NaN (spikes) on GPUs whose `tanh` does for large arguments; the surf zone's
  sand and distant foam are fainter.
- 290aafe: Water: the terrain's fragments skip the waves wherever none can show: on land, on the flat map, under the wave mesh (which draws the sea there itself), with the waves off, and farther than the largest cascade reaches (beyond about 6 m a pixel). They used to sample every cascade, the close-up ripples and the foam for every terrain pixel and weigh it away. On a CPU renderer the sea with its waves off costs a third less, the full storm about 15 % less; a view over land gains the most. `WaveSurface.sampleWaves` takes an optional `where` condition, and `fragmentPlane()` gives a terrain fragment's place on the wave plane.
- 8f8c5f0: Steadier sea near the camera: the wind's waves no longer calm by the distance to the shore without depth data (the
  shore map is redrawn as the camera moves, and the open sea changed with it); the surf's crests take their phase per
  pixel from the shore map instead of across the mesh's triangles (no more sawtooth, stretched crests), and fade out
  where the shore map is too coarse to place them.
- bbe6897: The GPU simulates 256 texels a side per cascade (`WAVE_COMPUTE_GRID_SIZE`), so the waves near the camera come from longer tiles and the tiling no longer shows; the CPU path keeps 64. The seafloor (`depthFromElevation`) no longer shows where the elevation model holds no depth (sea filled at 0 m), which drew sand rectangles off coasts.
- 4dc729f: Water: the wave mesh draws both sides, so a crest rising over a low camera shows the waves' underside (the sunlight through the water above, turquoise, `WaveMeshShading.underside`) instead of a black hole.
- 039bab4: Water: the GPU's inverse FFTs run one dispatch per direction, each workgroup transforming a whole row or column in workgroup memory with barriers between the stages, instead of one dispatch per stage through the storage buffers. A frame's simulation is 4 dispatches instead of 18, reading and writing the buffers about twelve times less (8 KiB of workgroup memory at 256 texels), and encodes in a fraction of the CPU time (the plugin's update falls under 1 ms where it took 2.7 ms on a slow CPU). The waves are the same.
